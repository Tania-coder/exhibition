Fyodor Dostoevsky

Writer · Russia · 1821 – 1881

The story

Fyodor Dostoevsky. Born in Moscow in 1821, in a hospital for the poor, where his father was a doctor. His father died in 1839. The family said his own serfs had killed him. Fyodor trained as a military engineer. He wanted to write.

In 1846, his first novel. Poor Folk. A poor copying clerk, Makar Devushkin, writes letters to a young seamstress. He buys her sweets he cannot afford. The critic Belinsky called the author a new Gogol. The idea: a small man in a worn coat has a whole inner life. A few weeks later, The Double. A clerk named Golyadkin meets himself in the street. The double is quicker, smoother, and takes his place at the office. Golyadkin ends in an asylum. The idea: a man who fears he is nobody is replaced by a better copy.

In April 1849 he was arrested. He belonged to a circle that read banned books. On the twenty-second of December he was led to Semyonovsky Square, with twenty others. They were told they would be shot. The first three were tied to posts. At the last moment, a courier arrived. The sentence was changed. Four years of hard labour in Omsk. Then army service.

Notes from the House of the Dead, 1860 to 1862. The nobleman Goryanchikov, who killed his wife, spends ten years in a Siberian prison. The baths. A Christmas play. A wounded eagle. The idea: a human being is a creature who gets used to everything.

In 1864 he lost his wife and his brother within four months. That year he wrote Notes from Underground. A retired clerk, forty years old, speaks from his cellar. I am a sick man. I am a spiteful man. He humiliates a young woman named Liza, and sends her away. The idea: a person will act against his own interest, only to prove he is free.

In 1866, Crime and Punishment. Raskolnikov, a poor student in St Petersburg, kills an old pawnbroker with an axe. Her sister walks in, and he kills her too. He had a theory: some people are allowed to step over the law. He does not feel like one of them. A young woman, Sonya, who sells herself to feed her family, reads him the story of Lazarus. He confesses. Eight years in Siberia. The idea: a theory that permits murder is answered by conscience.

That same autumn, he owed money to everyone. A publisher offered a contract: a new novel by the first of November, or he would lose the rights to his future work. Dostoevsky hired a stenographer, Anna Snitkina, twenty years old, and dictated The Gambler in twenty-six days. A tutor in a German casino town falls in love, and bets everything on roulette. The idea: he does not want the money. He wants the moment. He married Anna the next February.

Abroad, running from creditors, he wrote The Idiot. It appeared in 1868 and 1869. Prince Myshkin returns to Russia from a Swiss clinic. He is honest, gentle, and trusting. Everyone calls him an idiot. Two men love the same woman, Nastasya Filippovna. At the end, Rogozhin kills her, and the prince sits beside her body all night. His mind goes again. The idea: a truly good man, in a corrupt world, looks like an idiot.

In 1869 a student named Ivanov was murdered in Moscow by a revolutionary group. Dostoevsky read about it, and wrote Demons, in 1871 and 1872. Pyotr Verkhovensky gathers a small cell. To bind it together, he makes it kill one of its own, Shatov. The town burns. The charming Stavrogin, whom they all admire, hangs himself. The idea: ideas without conscience end in blood.

In 1875, A Raw Youth. Arkady is nineteen. He is the illegitimate son of a nobleman he has barely met. He tries to understand his father, and himself. Between the novels, he wrote a monthly, Diary of a Writer, alone. Essays, news, court trials. The idea: a boy without a father looks for one everywhere.

The Brothers Karamazov, 1879 to 1880. The father is murdered. The eldest son, Dmitri, is convicted. The killer is the servant Smerdyakov, who took a lesson from Ivan, the clever brother: if there is no God, everything is permitted. Ivan tells a poem, the Grand Inquisitor: Christ returns to earth, and he is arrested. The youngest brother, Alyosha, follows the monk Zosima. The idea: everyone is guilty before everyone, and responsible for everyone.

He drew all his life. In the margins of his manuscripts: faces. Gothic windows. Old handwriting, signatures, again and again. Next to plans for novels, and calculations.

In June 1880 he spoke about Pushkin in Moscow. People wept. Eight months later, on the ninth of February, 1881, he died in St Petersburg. He was fifty-nine. Tens of thousands followed the coffin. Fyodor Dostoevsky. Eighteen twenty-one, to eighteen eighty-one. You understand.
