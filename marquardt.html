Sven Marquardt

The story

Sven Marquardt. Born in East Berlin on the third of February, 1962. He grew up in Prenzlauer Berg. In the nineteen eighties, the local scene lived in squats and backyards.

Punk and new wave. They listened to Joy Division and The Cure. They painted No Future on their leather jackets. Police patrols followed them, and waited at the next corner. Papers were checked all the time. Once he was questioned alone. They said: turn your face to the wall. The army? He had decided he was not going.

He trained as a photographer. He worked for the magazines Sibylle and Das Magazin. His East Berlin was in black and white. On the fourth of November, 1989, half a million people gathered on Alexanderplatz. Five days before the Wall came down. For many East Germans, the end of the Wall meant losing part of their own identity. He stopped taking photographs.

The club Ostgut. Halls of a freight station on Mühlenstraße. In the late nineteen nineties, he stood at the door of Ostgut. In January 2003, the club closed. Then he picked up the camera again. For twelve years he photographed musicians for the Ostgut booking agency.

In 2004, Berghain opens, in a power station from 1953. He runs the door. Berghain. Kreuzberg, once in the West, and Friedrichshain, once in the East. One name for both. His own rule: never mention my photographs at the door.

His first photo book: Future's Past. Black and white portraits and scenes of Berlin. The title is borrowed from a line in a David Lynch series. In 2014, his autobiography: The Night Is Life. Written with Judka Strittmatter, published by Ullstein. Two hundred and twenty-four pages. East Berlin, punk, the Wall, the club.

Since 2015, he teaches at the Ostkreuz School of Photography. Weekends: the door. Weekdays: the camera. Born in the East. Made in Berlin. Still at the door. You understand.
