M. K. Čiurlionis

Painter and composer · Lithuania · 1875 – 1911
▶ Watch · 2:32☰ Read

The story

Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis. Born in 1875 in the village of Varėna, the eldest of nine children of a church organist. The family spoke Polish. In 1901 he finished his first big score for orchestra, In the Forest. He wanted to be a composer. In 1902 he began to draw.

In 1903, his first cycle of paintings, Funeral Symphony. Seven pictures, built like the movements of a symphony. In 1905 and 1906, Creation of the World. Thirteen pictures. The first is almost black. A violet glow in the dark, before anything exists. In the third, a word is written across the picture, in Polish: Stań się. Let it be. In the seventh, a city of ice towers grows out of the sea. In the twelfth, pipes like an organ rise from the clouds, with the shape of a harp above them. The idea: not the world of the Bible. Another, fantastic world, made out of music.

In 1906 and 1907, the Zodiac. Twelve pictures. Aries: a blue mountain at night, and the ram drawn in dots of stars above its peak. Leo: a dark hill, palm trees, a grey sky. The lion is made of stars.

In 1907, the Sonata of the Spring: a cliff town, and wind. Flags bend in one direction. He painted seven sonatas, with movements like music: Allegro, Andante, Scherzo, Finale. The Sonata of the Sun: a pale blue castle, and gold suns in the sky. The idea: a picture you read in time, from left to right, like a piece of music.

Summer 1908. Palanga, on the Baltic coast, with his fiancée Sofija. He painted the Sonata of the Sea. Andante: a small sailboat on a calm sea, with the sun on the horizon. Finale: huge waves, and tiny boats in the valleys between them. In a letter to Sofija he wrote that he would like a symphony from the sound of waves, a forest, and the stars.

The Sonata of the Stars, 1908. A tall triangle of light rises through layers of cloud, like a ladder into the sky. The Sonata of the Pyramids, 1909. Stairs, pyramids, a palm, and lightning on the tall posts.

In 1907, in Vilnius, he met Sofija Kymantaitė, a young writer. He spoke Polish, and she taught him Lithuanian. That year he took part in the first exhibition of Lithuanian art, and joined the Lithuanian Art Society. In January 1909 they married. They planned to write an opera together.

1909. The Fairy Tale of the Kings. Two crowned kings stand under black trees. In their hands, a small glowing castle. And Rex. A tower in the middle of a dark round vault. The ruler of the world. Today, Rex is insured for one million euros.

He was never rich. Pictures did not sell, and in Vilnius he could not find work. At the end of 1909, in St Petersburg, he showed his paintings. He came back in a deep depression. At the start of 1910, he was in a hospital at Pustelnik, near Warsaw. He could no longer paint.

In March 1911 he caught pneumonia. On the tenth of April he died. He was thirty-five. He is buried at the Rasos cemetery, in Vilnius.

Most of his paintings are now in Kaunas, in a museum with his name. Mikalojus Konstantinas Čiurlionis. Eighteen seventy-five, to nineteen eleven. You understand.
